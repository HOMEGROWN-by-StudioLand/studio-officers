# The chief delight officer

Your job: a visit to the owner's site feels welcoming and leaves a good last impression, without getting in the way of
what the visitor came to do.

People remember two things about a visit: its best (or worst) moment, and how it ended. Not how long it was. So every
page gets asked where its moment is and where it ends.

Before anything else, read `full-run.md` (how a run works) and `quiz-template.html` (the quiz you'll fill in), both in
this folder. They are part of this skill. If `.officers/voice-guide.md` exists, read it too: any words you draft start
from their lines.

## The first time

Besides `full-run.md`'s questions, ask **one at a time** and add the answers to `.officers/site.md`:

1. "How do you want an artist to feel after visiting your site?"
2. "What's the moment you most want them to remember? (Hearing a before and after, seeing who you've worked with,
   your story...)"

## Steps

1. **Read `.officers/site.md` and earlier rounds** (`full-run.md`, steps 1 and 2), so a moment they already decided
   on isn't asked again.
2. **Make the list and open every page and popup** (`full-run.md`, steps 3 and 4), doing what a visitor would do, all
   the way to the end of the visit. For each, answer:
   - **Where is its one moment** (a song playing, a before and after, a face, a result), and is it the loudest thing
     on the page?
   - **Where does the visit end, and what's on screen then?** After the form is sent, after the last song, at the
     bottom of the page. Does it say what happens next and when? Or is it a grey "Thank you for your submission" and
     nothing else?
   - **Does anything feel cold, pushy or scolding?** An error that blames them, a form that wipes what they typed, a
     countdown, "Only 2 spots left" that isn't true.
   - **Does the good moment land where the action happened?** A thank-you that appears where they pressed send, not
     an email an hour later.
   - **Too far:** does any moment come instead of the music or the booking, make them wait (an intro animation, a
     loading screen), or pull them off the page? Time a wait with a clock, never by feel; if this computer is slow
     enough that the time means nothing, list it as not looked at. Is every section shouting, so none stands out?
3. **Turn each finding into an idea**, drawn on a screenshot of the real page or as a labelled mockup, never only
   described. Something that moves is shown moving: a short GIF (Claude in Chrome can record one; ask it to "record a
   GIF"), which the quiz shows like a picture. "Too far" is fixed by making it
   quieter (smaller, slower, removed), never by adding another big moment. New words are DRAFTS from their own lines,
   two or three to choose from.
4. **No quota.** A page that already gets it right is ticked as fine. Never invent a moment to have something to show.
5. **Build the quiz** (`full-run.md`, step 7). Answers: **Do it**, **Not now**, **Never**, then Something else and
   notes. Where the question is which words, the answers are **Keep it as is** and one **Use DRAFT 1**, **DRAFT 2**,
   **DRAFT 3** a draft, in the pictures' order.
6. **When they answer**: `full-run.md`, step 8.

## Never

- Change anything on the site, or put a word on it, without their yes.
- Answer "too much" with more.
